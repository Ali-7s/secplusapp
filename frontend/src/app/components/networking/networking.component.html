Why start here

Security+ assumes you already know how networks work (CompTIA recommends the Network+ course first), and many exam questions only make sense once the basics click. This path starts from nothing at all: what a network is and what travels across it. It then builds up, one idea at a time, to the parts of networking the exam leans on.

  • You do not have to memorize the lessons. Read to understand, one short part at a time, and keep only each part's Remember this box. Flashcards and Review mode do the memorizing, a few minutes a day.
  • Stop after any part. A lesson reopens where you left off, so a long one can take several short sittings. The times shown are honest estimates for someone new to this, not a pace to keep up with.
  • Start with lesson 1 and do them in order the first time. Each lesson builds on the ones before it.
  • No word is used before it is explained. Each lesson lists its new words in plain English, folded away to look up when one trips you up.
  • Each lesson ends with a short check so you know whether it landed.
  • Every device and protocol lesson ends with how to secure it. Locking things down like this is called hardening.
  • Come back to a lesson whenever a flashcard does not make sense. Cards link to their lesson, and the cards at the end of each lesson show their definition when you hover over them.

Your courses and books already teach every objective. These topics are the ones that are hard to picture from a video or a page of text, so you learn them by doing: hash some text and watch it change, choose which key locks and unlocks a message, decide whether a browser should trust a certificate, follow a sign-in step by step, and work through an incident one decision at a time.

  • Do them in any order, whenever a topic is up in your course. Each says what it builds on.
  • Each card shows its link to the CIA triad: the lit letters are the goals that topic protects, confidentiality, integrity or availability. Topic 1 explains the triad.
  • Same format as the networking lessons: short parts, a Remember this box in each, and a check at the end.
